AI × Human Behavior

Robin Na

I am a PhD candidate in Information Technology at MIT, advised by Abdullah Almaatouq and Michiel Bakker.

I work on AI for safe and effective social decision support. My research involves building multi-agent AI systems for experimental design, literature synthesis, and human behavior simulation, while studying how to prevent collective misalignment.

Research

Designing Behavioral Profiles for Language Model Simulation of Out-of-Distribution Human Behavior

Which characteristics of a person help predict their behavior? We build an automated framework that constructs behavioral profiles for AI simulations, selecting the features that best predict collective human behavior in new experimental settings.

Robin Na, Kehang Zhu, Logan Cross, Michiel Bakker

Under review at Information Systems Research

Spotlight · NeurIPS 2026 User Simulation Workshop

02

Collective misalignment in multi-agent AI systems

How does collaboration among AI agents shape their conclusions? We build multi-agent research environments to study how AI scientists collect data, combine evidence, and make predictions and policy recommendations—and when their interactions lead to collective failures.

Misaligned Incentives and Tipping in Language Model Agent Networks

with Chanwoo Park, Michiel A. Bakker, Asu Ozdaglar

Working Paper

03

Evaluating scientific evidence

Which scientific findings help us predict behavior in new settings? We build AI systems that synthesize research literature and evaluate whether that evidence improves predictions of behavioral experiments.

Auditing Scientific Literatures with Large Language Models

Robin Na, Duncan J. Watts, Abdullah Almaatouq

Working Paper · Presented at the AI for Science Workshop at ICML

04

The Integration of Explanation and Prediction in Behavioral Science

How can behavioral science explain why people act as they do while also predicting what they will do next? We examine how explanatory and predictive approaches can inform one another.

Abdullah Almaatouq, Robin Na

Current Directions in Psychological Science, 35(3), 2026
